Lilo is a local browser. It has no account, no sync, no telemetry and no analytics, and the project runs no server: nothing you do in Lilo is sent to its developers. This page tells you what Lilo keeps on your Mac, every service it contacts on its own and how to turn each one off, and what WebKit and macOS do alongside it.

:::note
The canonical privacy statement is [`PRIVACY.md`](https://github.com/Alexays/Lilo/blob/main/PRIVACY.md) in Lilo's repository. This page follows it; if the two ever differ, `PRIVACY.md` is the reference.
:::

## What stays on your Mac

History, bookmarks, sessions, settings, site permissions, downloads and extension data live in SQLite databases and WebKit data stores under `~/Library/Application Support/Lilo`, separated per profile. Private Spaces use non-persistent WebKit data stores that are discarded when they close. Bookmarks are also indexed in Spotlight, on this Mac only, so they show up in Spotlight searches.

Lilo stores no passwords and has no password manager of its own: HTTP basic-auth credentials go to the macOS keychain.

Features that keep their own data, all on this Mac:

- **Auto-archive:** unpinned tabs left idle past a Space's delay (12 hours by default, or never) move to the Space's archive, which keeps their URL and title for 30 days, at most 200 per Space. Private Spaces are never written to disk.
- **Terminal tabs** run your shell as you. Lilo saves each terminal's screen and scrollback under `Terminals/` to restore it on relaunch, and deletes a saved screen not reopened for 30 days. Terminals of private Spaces are not saved.
- **Link rules** (Settings, links opened from other apps) are matched on your Mac to choose the Space or Little window a link opens in.
- **Peek and Little windows** open pages in the profile of the Space they belong to, like any tab. The Little window shortcut works from any app through a system hot key: Lilo needs no Accessibility permission and sees no other keystrokes.
- **Downloads** are stamped with macOS quarantine (the file's source URL, agent "Lilo"), so Gatekeeper checks them when you first open them.
- **Diagnostics** (Help > Diagnostics) are built on demand: **Copy Report** puts startup metrics and tab lifecycle counts on your clipboard, for you to paste where you choose. Lilo sends no crash report; macOS keeps its own under `~/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports`. When Lilo quits, it notes in the macOS log on this Mac where the request came from: a key or click, or the identifier of the app that asked, and the name of the last agent tool. It holds no URL, title, or key or text you typed.
- **Performance recordings** (Help > Record Performance Session) are written to your Downloads folder when you stop one, for you to share or delete: timings of Lilo's own work, CPU, memory, energy and disk use of its processes, the names of menu commands you used, the ids of your installed extensions, and your Mac's model and macOS version. They contain no URL, page title, name or text you typed. Lilo also keeps the hang and crash diagnostics macOS hands it (MetricKit) in its Application Support folder, to add them to the next recording; they never leave your Mac otherwise.
- **Bug reports:** Help > Report an Issue… opens GitHub's bug report form in a tab, filled in with your Lilo, WebKit and macOS versions. Nothing is sent until you submit the form yourself.

To see or clear what a profile stores, use Settings > Privacy. [Privacy controls](/guides/privacy-controls) walks through the settings.

## Requests Lilo makes on its own

Besides the pages you open, Lilo contacts these services:

| What | Where | When | How to turn it off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filter lists for content blocking | `easylist.to`, `ublockorigin.github.io`, and for the regional lists of your languages `easylist-downloads.adblockplus.org` and `raw.githubusercontent.com` | Every four hours at most | Turn off **Block ads and trackers** in Settings > Privacy (takes effect at the next launch); turn off **Regional ad lists** there |
| Search suggestions | Your search engine's suggestion endpoint (Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Brave) with what you type in the address bar | While typing | Turn off **Search suggestions** in Settings |
| Preload top hit | A site from your history you visit often, loaded with your cookies as if you had opened it | While you type its address in a blank tab, before you press Return; in a tab already showing a page, Lilo only opens a connection to that site | Turn off **Preload top hit** in Settings |
| Extension installs and updates | `clients2.google.com` (Chrome Web Store), with the ids and versions of your store extensions | On install, and an update check every five hours while store extensions are installed | Remove store extensions; turning off **Update extensions automatically** only stops installing new versions |
| Google sign-in for extensions (`chrome.identity.getAuthToken`) | `accounts.google.com`, with the extension's OAuth client id and scopes | Only when an extension asks you to sign in, in a panel you see | Remove the extension, or close the panel |
| Agents for Conversations | `cdn.agentclientprotocol.com` (the ACP registry, kept a day) and the icon addresses it lists; then the chosen agent itself: its package from npm or PyPI, or its archive from the address the registry lists, checked against the registry's SHA-256 | When you pick an agent or start a conversation | Leave **Conversations with your agents** off in Settings > Agents (off by default) |
| App updates (Sparkle) | `github.com` (the latest release's `appcast.xml`, then the update itself), with Lilo's version in the User-Agent; no system profile | Once an hour, from the first launch | Turn off **Install updates automatically** in Settings |

The Chrome Web Store sees Lilo as Chrome, so its install button (relabeled "Add to Lilo") works. [Extension compatibility](/reference/extension-compatibility) explains how. Local and debug builds never check for app updates.

## Requests WebKit and macOS make

Lilo uses the WebKit framework that ships with macOS, and with it these system services:

- **Fraudulent website warnings** (Safe Browsing) check visited addresses against Google's or Tencent's lists through Apple's privacy-preserving proxy.
- **Link tracking protection** strips known tracking parameters using a list macOS keeps up to date.
- **Page translation** runs on device with Apple's Translation framework.
- **Screen Time** receives the sites you visit outside Private Spaces, to report usage and enforce the limits set in System Settings > Screen Time.

## Tracking prevention and GPC

WebKit's cross-site tracking prevention (Intelligent Tracking Prevention) is on by default, as in Safari. A site embedded in another (an iframe, an image, a script) gets none of its own cookies there, so it cannot recognize you across the sites that embed it. An embed can ask for its cookies through the Storage Access API, and Lilo asks you first. Tracking prevention also deletes the cookies of sites it classifies as trackers, unless you used that site in the last 30 days: a click on a page counts as using its site. It keeps its statistics on your Mac only.

Cookies a site marks `Partitioned` still work in its frames, kept apart for each site embedding it, so embedded apps that sign in with them, such as claude.ai artifacts, keep working. With **Prevent cross-site tracking** turned off in Settings > Privacy, third-party cookies work as in Chrome, and a `Partitioned` cookie is stored unpartitioned. Turning it off clears its statistics, not the sites' data.

The content blocker lets bot-defense and captcha vendors through (PerimeterX/HUMAN, DataDome, Arkose Labs, hCaptcha, Cloudflare Turnstile): sites rely on their scripts to tell people from bots, and with them blocked they show a captcha on every visit. They can still see your visits to the sites using them.

Lilo sends the Global Privacy Control signal (`Sec-GPC`, `navigator.globalPrivacyControl`) by default, on macOS versions whose WebKit supports it. Turn it off with **Ask sites not to sell my data** in Settings > Privacy.

## Import from other browsers

Import reads, on your Mac, the data of Chrome, Arc, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi, Dia, Comet, Opera, Chromium, Firefox, Zen and Safari: bookmarks, open tabs and Spaces, history, cookies and the list of installed extensions. It never reads passwords or payment data. Chromium cookies are decrypted with the browser's "Safe Storage" keychain item, which macOS asks you to allow; Safari needs Full Disk Access. Imported extensions are installed again from the Chrome Web Store by id: their data stays in the other browser. See [Import sources](/reference/import-sources) for what comes from each browser.

## AI agents

Lilo has no AI of its own. Agent control is off until you turn on **Let agents use Lilo** in Settings > Agents. Agents connect through `lilo-mcp`, a local MCP server in the app bundle, over a Unix socket (`agent.sock` in Lilo's support folder) that only your user account can reach; nothing listens on the network.

- **Each client is approved once:** Lilo asks the first time it connects, and ties the approval to the program behind it (its code signature, or its path when unsigned). Settings lists approved clients and can revoke them.
- **Agents work in their own Agents Space**, in a separate "Agents" profile with none of your cookies, sign-ins or history, wiped at launch and when the last agent leaves. An agent can move to one of your profiles only after you allow that profile.
- **What agents can read:** the pages of their own tabs (text, accessibility tree, screenshots, console, network, cookies of the sites those tabs show). They never see your other tabs, and never see or act on a private Space. Their `fetch` tool never returns `Set-Cookie` or `Authorization`.
- **Approval gate:** unless you turn it off, Lilo asks before an agent clicks or presses Enter on something that sends, pays, buys, orders, deletes, transfers, publishes or signs up (**Allow Once**, **Always Allow on This Site**, **Don't Allow**), and before it sends a request other than GET or HEAD with your cookies. Sites you always allow are listed in Settings. Handing a page one of your files always asks, whatever the setting.

The gate is a safety net, not a sandbox: code an agent runs in a page can act without asking. [Agent safety](/about/agent-safety) explains what the gate covers and what it does not.

## Tab sharing

When a page asks to share your screen, Lilo's picker lists your tabs. The tab you pick is captured inside Lilo and handed to that page only, video only; the sidebar marks the shared tab. **Window or Screen...** opens the macOS picker instead. When the page also asks for sound, **Share audio too** adds what your Mac plays to a window or screen share, leaving out Lilo's own sound, so the voices of a call are never sent back into it. Extensions using `chrome.tabCapture` or `chrome.desktopCapture` go through the same pickers.

## Extensions

Extensions you install run with the permissions they request, which Lilo shows before installing. What an extension does with your data is governed by that extension's own privacy policy.
